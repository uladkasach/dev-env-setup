# domain.term.choice.reason: start

## .etymology

adopted from the human, not coined. asked of a command line on 2026-09-06:

> *"should this say audio.record.start? instead of open? open sounds like listen;
> rhx audio.record.open --purpose demo"*

⇒ so `start` is a **word that fell out of a misread**, which is the strongest provenance a
term can have here: it was not chosen for elegance, it was reached for by a reader who had
just parsed the extant word wrong.

## .the term was born of a DISPUTE, not of a pattern

📜 **2026-09-06.** `open` was the extant word across three families — `duct.open`,
`term.open`, `audio.record.open` — and it had never been itemized. the human's question
above found no glossary entry to answer from, so `term=open` was written that hour, with
the dispute recorded as OPEN inside it.

the mechanic's counter argued to keep `open` on three grounds. the human ruled the other
way, in two words:

> *"audio.record.start instead"*

⇒ the outcome was not a straight swap. the dispute had surfaced that `start` and `open`
name **two concepts** — an act, and the state it produces — so the resolution was
`howto.domain-term-disputes`'s second outcome: *the disputed word prevails → it becomes a
new term of its own.* this cluster is that term.

## 🛑 .the lesson worth more than the word — a defense that enumerates has conceded

the counter's own first line was:

> *"`start` names an INSTANT. `open` names the state that instant produces."*

it wrote that to argue for one word. it is the **enumeration that proves there are two**,
and one word across two senses is `rule.forbid.domain-term-ambiguity` verbatim.

⇒ so the general form, and it is the reusable half:

> **when you defend one word by a description of the two senses it carries, you have
> already answered the question against yourself.**

`rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name` asks for the list of instances BEFORE the word is
picked. here the list existed and sat inside the defense, where nobody read it as a list.
the rule's own failure mode — *"a word that covers every row is the genus"* — arrived in a
costume: the word covered both rows, and that read as generality rather than as overload.

## .the cost the counter mispriced

its third ground was that the misread is *"SELF-CORRECTING, and cheap"*: a human who runs
the command in hope of playback sees a mic probe and a live meter within three seconds, so
loses no take.

⇒ true, and it is the wrong quantity. it prices **one recovery** and never prices the
read. a label that must be corrected on each encounter taxes every future reader; only the
first correction is cheap, and there is no last one.

`def.ergonomic` states the bar directly — a label may read one way only, and *"a result
that invites a re-read"* already fails, whatever the recovery costs afterward.

## .disputes

### dispute: begin — raised 2026-09-06 — status: RESOLVED (keep `start`)
- raised.by  = the mechanic
- claim      = `begin` is the plainer english, and reads gentler in prose about a
               conversation with a grandmother
- counter    = a skill name is a **command**, not prose. `start` is the word every other
               tool on this box uses for the same act (`systemctl start`, `docker start`),
               so a human meets it already known. `begin` would be this repo's private word
               for a universal one — `rule.forbid.domain-term-inconsistency` against the
               wider domain rather than against ourselves
- resolution = keep `start`; record `begin` as a forbidden synonym

### dispute: capture — raised 2026-09-06 — status: RESOLVED (keep `start`)
- raised.by  = the mechanic
- claim      = `capture` is what the skill's own help text says it does, and it is the
               audio domain's real word
- counter    = it names the RESULT of the whole take, not the instant the take begins. a
               `capture` that returns at once, with the take still empty, is a false claim
               about what happened. and `record` already carries that sense in the same
               operation name, so `audio.record.capture` would say one word twice
- resolution = keep `start`. the help text may still say *"capture a take"* — prose is
               free to name the outcome; the OPERATION names the act
