# domain.term.choice.reason: holder

## .etymology

from the shape m.9 names: *one claim, two readers, and the cheaper reader is the one
that stays.* the repo said "two readers" for months and the word was wrong — a READER
consumes the fact, and what drifts is the place the fact is WRITTEN. so the drift needed
its own noun, and `holder` is the one that carries "this place HOLDS the fact, and
somebody trusts it alone".

chosen over:

| rejected | why |
|---|---|
| `copy` | says the two are identical, which is their state only until the first edit. the whole term exists to name the moment they stop being copies |
| `instance` | reads as an occurrence of a pattern, not as an authority somebody consults |
| `site` | already means "a place in code where a call appears" here, and a call site is not a holder — a hundred call sites of one clamp have exactly ONE holder |
| `duplicate` | prejudges the fix as "delete one". sometimes the right fix is two holders that a check holds to agreement |

## .why `site` is the sharpest rejection

they are opposite counts of the same code, and to conflate them inverts the diagnosis:

```
__term_as_ssh_host  — 1 holder  (the grammar), N call sites
the ssh-host grammar — 2 holders (termwork's clamp, and the inline fallback
                       in git.grove.auth.github.set), which is the real m.9
```

⇒ a hundred call sites of one clamp is HEALTH. two clamps behind one grammar is the
defect. one word for both loses the distinction the term was minted for.

## .disputes

### dispute: reader — raised 2026-09-01 — status: RESOLVED (both terms stand, for different roles)
- raised.by = the author of this cluster
- claim = m.9 is already written as "one claim, TWO READERS" across a dozen briefs, and a
  new noun risks a synonym for a settled word
- counter = they name opposite ends. a `reader` CONSUMES a fact and a `holder` STATES
  one, and m.9's actual failure is two holders — the readers are usually fine, each
  faithful to the holder it consults. the r8 B1 measurement is exactly this: two
  carriers HELD one policy, and each carrier's reader was internally correct
- resolution = both terms stand. `reader` keeps its sense (a check, a consumer of state);
  `holder` names the place a fact is written. m.9's wording in extant briefs is left
  alone — it is a measurement's record, and a sweep of settled prose is the blocker
  `rule.prefer.wickup-touched-prose` names

## .evidence

| measured | the fact | its holders | what drifted |
|---|---|---|---|
| r8 B1, 2026-09-01 | which rsync stream names a refused member | `git.grove.push` (stdout, seen red) and `git.grove.pull` (stderr) | the pull's guard was unreachable on every box |
| r9 B2, 2026-09-01 | whether `.git` crosses a grove boundary | `git.grove.push` declares it never does; `git.grove.pull` carries it back | the inbound half held no part of the outbound policy |
| r8 B2, 2026-09-01 | that a skill which relays grove output loads the sink | three skills carried the block; a fourth carried none | the fourth was invisible to every reader of the three |
| 2026-08-30 | a tpm pin's value | `2.8.tmux`'s `_.sh` and two independent verify readers | both readers went red against correct code once the pin moved |

⇒ the row that matters most is r8 B1, because the EVIDENCE was already in the repo. one
holder had been measured — seen red against a planted stale file — and the other had
been assumed. **when a fact has two holders, one of them has usually been proven; go
read that one.**
