## .the ask, as the human framed it

> get a brief to persist as durable lessons […] that was litigated, so the full history is
> retained. typically, invariants are litigated, so it should be a part of that frame.
> invariants are either ordained by nature or nurture; either its in the nature of the domain,
> or its what we've nurtured into the domain.

## .why — the gap this closes

the learner role already has two homes for a lesson:

| kind | home |
|------|------|
| a general insight | a brief |
| a **term** choice, with its forbidden synonyms and its disputes | `domain.terms/term=<x>._.choice.*` |

but an **invariant** is neither. it is not one word, so it has no term cluster; and it is not a
loose insight, so a plain brief loses the part that matters most about it — **the argument that
produced it**.

so today an invariant gets written as a `rule.require.*` that states the conclusion, and the
litigation behind it survives only if whoever wrote it happened to narrate it. that is a coin
flip, and the cost of a lost argument is that it gets re-litigated from scratch — or, worse,
quietly reversed by someone who only ever saw the conclusion.

### the evidence, from one day

on 2026-07-29 this repo litigated at least five invariants. every one was argued, and every one
nearly lost its argument:

| the invariant | what it was litigated against |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| `grove.provision` is the ONLY entrypoint | four separate duplicate drivers, each of which felt like a new *concept* |
| the name is `upgrade` BECAUSE the inventory is idempotent | a prior brief argued the exact opposite, from the same premise |
| `--into X` puts the CONTENT at X | rsync's own slash-at-the-end convention said otherwise |
| **two transports are fine; disagreement is not** | a robot moved to delete rsync outright; the human stopped it |
| a duct is reached only through its skills | ~10 raw `ssh` calls, waved through by the allowlist |

the fourth is the sharpest, and it is the one that proves the frame:

> a robot read "two implementations drift" (true, and hard-won over four incidents) and applied
> it to `git.grove.push`'s rsync+tar pair. the human: *"wait. is rsync the main usecase though?
> if so, i get the fallback. just document it clearly why we need both, so we dont relitigate."*
>
> the rule was right and the application was wrong, because the rule's SCOPE had never been
> written down. two lists of *knowledge* drift; two *carriers* of one payload do not. that
> distinction existed only in the human's head.

**"so we dont relitigate" is the requirement.** a conclusion with no argument attached is a
conclusion that will be re-argued, and the second argument will not have the evidence the first
one had.

## .the frame — nature vs nurture

the human's taxonomy, and it is the load-bear idea of this dream. every invariant is one of two
kinds, and **which kind it is determines whether it may ever be revisited**:

### ordained by NATURE — true of the domain itself

it holds because of what the domain IS. no decision could have gone the other way, and a future
traveler cannot trade it away.

- *a machine is never blank.* every box ships a login shell, a PATH, `/etc/skel` dotfiles. so no
  run ever puts a tree on an empty machine — it always raises an extant one. this is why
  `upgrade` is the accurate verb and `install` is the one that misleads. **not a preference; a
  fact about machines.**
- *an idempotent operation's re-run IS its update.* so a separate `upgrade` verb beside an
  idempotent `install` would name one act twice.
- *a step that puts a question to a human does not converge.* it stops. convergence and
  interrogation are mutually exclusive by definition.

> a nature invariant may be **discovered wrong** — you misread the domain — but it may never be
> **traded away** for convenience. the appeal is to evidence about the domain, never to taste.

### nurtured INTO the domain — true because we chose it, and choose it still

it holds because of a decision, usually one paid for in incidents. another team could
defensibly have chosen otherwise. it still binds us, and the reason it binds is the scar tissue.

- *one entrypoint, `grove.provision._.sh`.* nature permits many entrypoints; four incidents
  taught us they drift. nurtured.
- *`--into X` means the CONTENT lands at X.* rsync's convention says otherwise and is
  internally consistent. we chose the sense a human gives the word. nurtured.
- *two carriers are fine; a layout disagreement is not.* nature has no opinion on carrier count.
  nurtured, and nearly reversed the same day it was stated.
- *`main | tree`, never `this`.* nurtured — the human closed it: *"its tree; we should use tree"*.
- *reach a grove only through its skills.* nature permits raw ssh; five forfeited guarantees
  taught us not to. nurtured.

### why the split is the whole point

it tells a future traveler **what kind of argument would even be admissible**:

| the invariant is… | to overturn it you must show… | what is NOT admissible |
|-------------------|-------------------------------|------------------------|
| nature | the domain was misread | "this is inconvenient" |
| nurture | the incidents no longer apply, or the cost has changed | "i would have chosen differently" |

without the label, every invariant gets argued as if it were nurture — negotiable on taste —
even the ones that are simply facts about machines. that is exactly how the
`install` → `upgrade` argument went wrong the first time: a nature fact (a box is never blank)
was weighed as a preference between two words.

## .what the brief should carry, per litigated invariant

1. **the claim** — one line, in the imperative
2. **the kind** — `nature` or `nurture`, and one sentence on why that kind
3. **the litigation** — who argued what, against what, and what settled it. quote the human
   verbatim where they closed it; the exact words carry the reason
4. **the evidence** — the incidents that bought it. dates, and what each one cost
5. **the scope** — what the invariant does NOT cover. this is the field the rsync incident
   proves is load-bear, and the field a conclusion-only rule always omits
6. **the counter-argument, kept alive** — the strongest case against it, stated fairly. a rule
   that has buried its opposition cannot be re-examined honestly
7. **what would overturn it** — the admissible argument, per the table above

fields 5, 6, and 7 are the ones a `rule.require.*` file does not have today, and they are the
three the re-litigation actually needs.

## .where it should live

a peer of `domain.terms/`, because it is the same move one level up — a term cluster records why
a WORD was chosen; this records why a CONSTRAINT was adopted:

```
.agent/repo=.this/role=any/briefs/domain.invariants/
  ├─ .readme.md                                  # the rules that rule the dir, + the nature/nurture split
  ├─ invariant=<slug>._.choice._.md              # say: claim, kind, scope
  └─ invariant=<slug>._.choice.reason.md         # ref: litigation, evidence, counter-argument, what would overturn
```

the `choice._.` cluster shape is already proven by `domain.terms/`, and reuse means the say/ref
boot split (lean always-loaded root, deep on-demand reason) comes for free.

## .the learner-role change this implies

`im_an.obsessive_learner.md` lists its triggers; a **litigated invariant** is absent from them
today. it should be there, as a third kind beside the two extant ones:

| trigger | lands as |
|---------|----------|
| a concept was a surprise / hard to discover | a brief |
| a domain term was discovered or debated | a `domain.terms/` cluster |
| **an invariant was litigated** | **a `domain.invariants/` cluster** |

and the onStop sweephook that nudges `learn.domain.terms` should nudge this too — the same
"tend it while the round is warm" argument applies, and for the same reason: the argument fades
faster than the conclusion.
